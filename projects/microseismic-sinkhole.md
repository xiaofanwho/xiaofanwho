# Sinkhole Detection at MicroSeismic (KarstAlert)

**Case studies:** [MicroSeismic Blog](https://www.microseismic.com/news-events/microseismic-blog/aliens-or-geology-unraveling-floridas-sinkholes-mystery/) • [WUSF: What Mosaic Is Doing With Its Gypstack](https://www.wusf.org/environment/2021-06-17/what-mosaic-is-doing-with-its-gypstack-to-prevent-another-piney-point-disaster)

MicroSeismic Inc. built KarstAlert®, a real-time, 24/7 passive monitoring system that listens for the microseismic signals produced deep underground as limestone dissolves and voids form — giving an early warning before a sinkhole collapses at the surface. It grew out of the company's original downhole microseismic method, developed for monitoring oil & gas hydraulic fracturing, adapted into a new business line for sinkhole and ground-stability monitoring in Florida's karst terrain and at industrial sites like Mosaic's phosphate gypstacks.

## My role

I was one of a handful of engineers who helped build this new technology at MicroSeismic Inc. (2016–2020), evolving the company's method from surface to downhole microseismic monitoring and ultimately into KarstAlert.

- Contributed to R&D adapting downhole seismic sensing and data processing techniques for continuous sinkhole and subsurface void detection
- Led QC/QA for the software, designing and running multiple tests with cross-functional teams to validate the new product before release
- Optimized operational workflows and improved ETL efficiency by configuring modular data components, so continuous seismic data could be processed and monitored in near real time

## Why it mattered

Florida's limestone geology means groundwater slowly dissolves the rock underground, leaving hollow spaces that eventually collapse without warning. Industrial sites like Mosaic's phosphate gypstacks face the same risk with much higher stakes — a 2016 sinkhole beneath Mosaic's New Wales facility sent over 200 million gallons of polluted water into the aquifer. By listening for the subtle underground shifts that precede a collapse, KarstAlert gave operators — and communities living above karst terrain — a chance to catch the problem while there was still time to act.
